# Decision ledger: installed toolkit vs ECC

ECC pin: tag `v2.2.1`, commit `5064474d4d762dc9640234a41617cccb79185cec`. Installed corpus: `skill-library` at `34cd9f10836707d3d760d081594133986654f9ae`, plus the global hooks, `CLAUDE.md` and `settings.json` as of 2026-09-17.

Generated by `notes/make-ledger.py`. Verdict tokens and deciding criteria are the judges' words with X and Y translated to `[installed]` and `[ecc]`. Lanes, risk lines, targets, effort and owners are orchestrator proposals by fixed rules, for Graham to rule on at Gate B; none is a verdict.

## Limits of this evidence

- Judging was **context-clean, not blind**: judges (Sonnet) read neutral extraction reports, twice, with the order swapped, and never saw source files or this project's documents. 13 of 14 judgments say they did not try or had no basis to recognize a side. One did guess: `judgments/review/judge-YX.md` wrote that one side's references to a named model, a phase number and a run log "suggest a purpose-built internal agent-skill system" and the other reads like "independently-authored Claude Code subagents/skills", then said it judged on content regardless. Every judge's context also held the owner's email address and a cwd path containing `gfm` and `harness-eval`; neither ties a side to a letter.
- The orchestrator saw the X/Y assignment of two slots during Phase 3 (checker output beside the run log). It does not judge.
- **All 14 verdict tokens are `merge`.** Agreement on the token does not mean the two judges named the same items: the per-item files record both judges' class for every item, and they differ on the counts shown in each row's Risk cell.
- The gatekeeper challenge rows are **labeled**: one side was written to the bar it is judged against.
- Bench results come from **synthetic events fed to hook scripts** in a no-network container, one fresh container per event, not from hooks firing in a Claude session. Fresh state means ECC's first-use gate denied every Bash and Edit event, benign ones included; see `bench/summary.md`.
- Fixture results are **fixture-derived** and say nothing about real repositories. The fixture showed no difference between arms that held in every run; Skill use was not observable; ECC items ran in 1 of 3 runs of their arm; the bare arm still sees Claude Code's bundled review skills. See `fixtures/review/summary.md`.
- Static slots: `build-loop`, `memory`, `orchestration`. Their verdicts rest on reading only (`judgments/build-loop/EVIDENCE.md`, `judgments/memory/EVIDENCE.md`, `judgments/orchestration/EVIDENCE.md`).
- `gates-pre-work` has **no verdict**: one side's extraction failed the name-leak check 3 of 3 times and Graham ruled against a fourth attempt. Its only evidence is the hook bench.
- Per-item rows map a judge's `item-N` to a source file **by position** in each slot's `items.tsv` under `slots/`; count and type sequence match for every side (`notes/item-order-check.py`), but no item was matched one by one.
- **`SUPERIOR SUBSTITUTE` is direction-ambiguous in these judgments.** Some judges wrote it on the better item, others on the item that has a better counterpart ("SUPERIOR SUBSTITUTE exists in ...", recorded as `SUPERSEDED`). The assembly does not guess: an ECC item counts as named to take only when a judge classed it `COMPLEMENT` or `INGESTIBLE FRAGMENTS`. Every such cell is printed in `ledger-items/AMBIGUOUS-CELLS.md`, including the one row a judge wrote for a range of items (ECC `orchestration` items 9 to 13: "REDUNDANT among themselves ...; relative to Y, COMPLEMENT", recorded `REDUNDANT` by the first-token rule, so those five are not counted as named to take; the other judge classed them `DISCARD`).
- **Judges were not always consistent with themselves.** The consistency check found 6 ECC items whose per-item row says `COMPLEMENT` while the same judge's Verdict prose drops them (`notes/verdict-prose-conflicts.tsv`). The patch files list them under their own heading; nothing was repaired.
- Fixed rules behind the proposal columns: lane is `hook-gate` when at least half of the ECC items a judge named to take are hooks or scripts, `incubator` otherwise, `reference-only` when none is named; effort is S under 150 source lines, M under 600, L otherwise; target plugin is the one holding most of the slot's installed items.
